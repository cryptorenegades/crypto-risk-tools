How to use the calculator

  1. Pick the direction, then enter the entry, the stop where the idea fails, and the target where you plan to take the position off.
  2. Read the ratio. 1 : 2 means the target is twice as far from the entry as the stop.
  3. Add your amount at risk (optional) to see the plan in dollars.
  4. Use it as a filter. Decide the minimum ratio you accept before the trade, and pass on setups that do not meet it.

How the risk reward formula works

  1. R = (target - entry) ÷ (entry - stop) for a long
  2. R = (entry - target) ÷ (stop - entry) for a short
  3. Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + R)

Why that last line holds: if every trade risks 1 unit to make R units, and a share p of trades reach the target, the result per trade is p × R - (1 - p) × 1. Set that to zero and solve: p = 1 ÷ (1 + R). It describes the plan. It does not say how likely the target is.

Worked example

Long at $100, stop $95, target $110, $500 at risk
  1. Risk per unit: 100 - 95 = $5. Reward per unit: 110 - 100 = $10.
  2. R = 10 ÷ 5 = 2, so the ratio is 1 : 2.
  3. Break-even win rate: 1 ÷ (1 + 2) = 33.3%.
  4. With $500 at risk, the stop costs $500 and the target adds $1,000. Over nine trades like this with three at the target and six at the stop: 3 × 1,000 - 6 × 500 = $0, before fees.

What the ratio does not tell you

The ratio says nothing about how likely the target is. A distant target improves the ratio on paper, but price has further to travel to reach it. A tight stop improves it too, but normal movement reaches a tight stop more often. Treat the ratio as a planning filter, not a forecast, and remember that fees and slippage push the real break-even slightly higher.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the target to make the ratio work.The target should come from the chart, a level where you expect price to react, not from the ratio you want to see.
  • Tightening the stop to improve the ratio.A stop closer than the idea needs gets hit by ordinary movement. Size the position down instead.
  • Ignoring fees on small moves.When the reward is only a fraction of a percent, fees can take a large share of it and raise the real break-even.
  • Reading the break-even win rate as a promise.It is arithmetic for planning. It says nothing about your results, or anyone else's.
