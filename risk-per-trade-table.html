How to read the risk per trade table

Find your account size and read across. Each number is what one losing trade costs if the stop fills where you planned. At 1% of $10,000, that is $100.

Risk per trade is not the position size. A $100 risk with a stop 5% below the entry is a $2,000 position. The position size calculator turns the dollars at risk into a position.

Choosing a risk per trade

Many disciplined traders keep risk per trade between 0.5% and 2% of the account. The lower end suits newer traders and approaches with long losing streaks. Whatever you choose, choose it before you look at the setup and keep it fixed for a set number of trades, so one decision is not made in the heat of the moment.

Recalculate from the current balance. After a drawdown, the same 1% is fewer dollars, and that is exactly what slows the damage. The drawdown recovery calculator shows why a shallow drawdown matters so much.

Worked example

$25,000 account at 1.5% risk
  1. Amount at risk: 25,000 × 1.5% = $375 per trade.
  2. Entry $80, stop $76: $4 per unit.
  3. Units: 375 ÷ 4 = 93.75, a position worth 93.75 × 80 = $7,500.
  4. Fifteen losses in a row at 1.5% would take the account down 20.28%, as the streak table above shows.

Common mistakes

  • Using last year's balance.Size from the capital in the account today, not from a past peak or from open gains that can still reverse.
  • Rounding up just this once.A 1% plan with the occasional 5% trade behaves like a 5% plan when the losing streak arrives.
  • Treating the table as a position size.These are dollars at risk. The position size depends on how far away the stop is.
  • Forgetting fees on small accounts.On a $1,000 account, a $5 risk can be a large share of the fees on a small position. Check the cost before the trade.
